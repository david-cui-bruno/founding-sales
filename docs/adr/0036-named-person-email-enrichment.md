# 0036: Campaign emails go to a named decision-maker with a provider-verified personal address

## Status and scope

Proposed November 2 2026 for David's approval; **amended by [ADR0037](0037-own-site-people-extraction.md): the Apollo adapter, its `apollo-api-key` secret and two-stage injection are retired (Apollo is not affordable); the provider interface, acceptance rule, custody and credit cap stay for a future paid source.** Amends [ADR0035](0035-prepared-company-email-admission.md). David's direction: role addresses (`info@`, `office@`) are not acceptable for the campaign; each email goes to a named owner, president, principal, managing or designated broker or property manager, at a verified personal address from a people-data provider. The `info@` campaign is not going live. It grants no sending, Gmail capture, LinkedIn action or contact; owner sending stays OFF; the campaign's approval, caps (25 lifetime, 5 a day), holds, expiry and the shared send gate are unchanged. Nothing here calls a provider until Root installs the key and an admin turns the setting on.

## Decision

**Provider interface.** `PeopleProvider.findDecisionMaker({domain, companyName})` answers one person (provider id, first and last name, title, email, `verified | unverified | unavailable`, match confidence) and the credits used, or a failure (`auth_failed`, `quota_exhausted`, `rate_limited`, `unavailable`, `invalid_response`) that says whether the paid call may have been charged. Adapters never retry and never expose a body. **Apollo** is the first adapter (`apps/worker/src/providers/apolloPeople.ts`): People API Search (`mixed_people/api_search`, 0 credits, no emails) over the company's host with the decision-maker title list, ranked here (owner, founder, president, principal, CEO; then managing/designated broker, broker of record; then director of property management and property manager) and skipping assistant, leasing, maintenance, accounting and similar titles and people Apollo says have no email; then ONE People Enrichment (`people/match` by id and domain; about one credit when an email returns, none when nothing matches). Header `x-api-key`, key `FSS_APOLLO_API_KEY` only. **Hunter** is the intended second adapter (`domain-search` with `type=personal&decision_maker=true`, one call); it needs only a new file. Search filters by title only: the seniority filter's combination semantics with titles are unconfirmed, and the search is free, so the adapter over-fetches and ranks.

**Acceptance is the domain's rule, not the adapter's** (`acceptablePerson`): the provider status is `verified` (Apollo `verified`; `likely to engage`, `unverified` and a missing or locked email are dropped), the match is high or medium, the last name is real (a masked "Bl***e" is dropped), the enriched title still ranks, and the address parses, is not a consumer domain, is not a role or system mailbox (`info`, `office`, `contact`, `admin`, `sales`, `support`, `leasing`, and similar; a closed list in `peopleProvider.ts`) and sits at the company's own host. The database repeats the last two: a stored person is lower case, verified and at the row's host. A refused answer is recorded as a reason with no personal data.

**Cost, never paying twice, daily cap.** Per company: search 0 credits, enrichment at most 1. The company's spend custody row in `outreach_people_lookups` (migration 0160; one per candidate and per host and provider) is inserted and committed BEFORE the paid call with the maximum cost (1 credit), under the `people_enrichment` setting's read lock (re-read at every reservation, so a disable or a cap decrease stops the next paid call) and a workspace advisory lock that first checks that the business date's reserved and spent credits plus 1 fit the `people_enrichment` cap (default 30; business date in the workspace zone). The result settles the row: `found`, `none` (nobody, or an answer refused above), `failed` (a refusal or a failed free search: nothing was charged, credits 0, tried again after one hour, the only retry), or `unknown` (the paid call may have been charged, a thrown provider, or a reservation that never settled: it keeps its credit and is never retried). Everything else, including `none`, is never retried automatically; a row is thirty days current, and re-enriching a company is an explicit operator deletion. Any provider failure halts the pass; the cap stops it.

**Billing follows Apollo's charge rule, not the email.** One People Enrichment call costs one credit for demographics or an email (no phone, waterfall or personal-email reveal is requested, so one is the most); Apollo says email credit usage does not depend on `match_confidence`, so any 2xx answer that contains a person object, at any confidence, costs one credit and only an explicit no-match (no person) costs nothing. `apolloPeople.ts` therefore reports one credit for any returned person. A high-confidence match with no email is recorded as `none` (`unverified`) with its one credit, counted against the daily cap. Where charging is undecidable the pass charges: a failed or unreadable response after the paid request left is `unknown` and keeps its reserved credit; a refusal (401/402/403/429) or a 4xx validation rejection answers before any processing and stays uncharged.

**Custody is independent of person data.** Migration 0160 is two tables. `outreach_people_lookups` is the body-free custody (opaque candidate id, host, provider, state, reason, credits, business date, window): no personal field and no foreign key to the candidate, so no erasure deletes it and neither the host fence nor the daily-cap ledger can be erased into a second payment; the application may not delete it. `outreach_people_contacts` is the provider person copy (provider id, name, title, address, confidence) of a `found` lookup: candidate deletion cascades it, and ordinary CRM contact and Firm deletion remove the copies of the companies admitted into that contact or Firm (or whose address is a deleted route) in both the preview (it lists the removal and the retained custody) and the commit. The lookup stays `found`, so the company is neither re-enriched nor re-admitted (the admission lineage trigger needs the person copy). A reservation's fence is the host, so the selection also excludes hosts that already hold custody before the batch limit. A placeholder or role first name (`Office`, `Unknown`, the same predicate that blanks `{first_name}`) is refused as `no_name` before a person is recorded, and a vice president is never read as a president: only a VP of property management ranks (below owners), and a finance, marketing or operations VP is refused. The prepared evidence deadline (the earliest admitted page, capture or source deadline) bounds an admission proof together with the person's expiry and the campaign's.

**Two-stage secret.** ECS refuses a task whose secret has no value, and a worker with a failing replacement leaves production with none. The `apollo-api-key` container is always created empty; the worker is handed it only when `worker_reads_apollo_api_key` (committed false in `infra/roots/production/main.tf`) is true. Stage one: apply with it false. Root then installs the value (`aws secretsmanager put-secret-value --secret-id fss-prod/apollo-api-key --secret-string file:///dev/stdin`). Stage two: a separate one-line change flips it to true and deploys. No targeted apply; the worker already starts without the variable (no key, no job).

**Gate and selection.** The `outreach.person_enrich` job (source `people-enrichment`, every ten minutes per workspace, `outbound_fence`, bulk lane) runs only where the worker has the key AND an admin has set `people_enrichment` to `{ "enabled": true, "dailyCredits": N }` (0 to 200; default off, 30), so the first 25 can be previewed before any campaign is approved; the Apollo key alone does nothing. It takes prepared companies in the prepared list's order (the prepared brief, one per host, a resolvable zone, no existing Firm, the shared business-name resolver) but no published address; it takes at most ten lookups a pass and enriches the approved campaign's regions, or every state while none exists. The 400-record scan limit of ADR0035 applies, so companies already looked up still occupy scan positions.

**Admission.** The ADR0035 pass now selects prepared companies that have a current `found` person for exactly the candidate revision, with the same refusals (recorded invalid check, existing Firm, zone, a stop on the person's address). The DNS MX check runs on the person's address as before. Admission creates the Firm (always new), a contact named for the person with their title and a `research_provider` route (association confidence 0.95 high, 0.85 medium), then the existing route validation. `source_kind` stays `prepared_capture`; a second lineage trigger (0160, beside 0158's check of the run and the address check) also proves a current found person with this address and the contact's name. `outreach_email_sources` has no foreign key on its observation, so the provider contact row's id is stored as the observation and `person:<provider>` as the block. The page-published address path (`preparedEmailRoute`) is deleted.

**Templates.** `{first_name}` is the contact's first name and is absent (a missing-variable hold) for a role contact (`Office`, `Team`) or a placeholder. Email 1 opens `Hi {first_name},`; emails 2-5 have no greeting; subjects keep `{firm_name}`. `{contact_first_name}` is unchanged.

**Preview.** `POST /outreach/email-campaigns/prepared-preview` returns, in admission order, the company, website, city/state, candidate and host, the person (name, title, address, status, match confidence, provider, when observed, credits spent) and the stored DNS check, and how many companies still await enrichment. Still a read-only transaction with no provider call, lock or write.

## Cost estimate (assumptions, not measurements)

No live call was made. If 30-40% of looked-up companies yield an acceptable, verified person, 25 admissions need about 60-85 lookups; an enrichment charges only when an email returns, so assume 50-70% of lookups cost 1 credit: about 30-60 credits, never more than the lookup count (60-85) because each lookup reserves at most one. At the 30 credit a day cap that is two to three business days. Spending continues at up to the cap per day while the setting is on; an admin turns it off, or sets `dailyCredits` to 0. The Apollo plan, credit price and whether the free tier allows enrichment are unconfirmed.

## Questions for David (not decided here)

Apollo plan and credit budget; Apollo's API terms and data-use limits for cold outreach; CAN-SPAM identification and opt-out duties are the sender's, and Apollo gives no consent or provenance guarantee (the docs do not say whether an address was found or inferred); GDPR/UK: Apollo withholds some personal addresses for people in GDPR regions and the campaign is US-only, but residence is unknown; whether `verified` is sufficient (it is the vendor's claim, not a delivery guarantee; the free DNS check still runs and bounces and replies remain the signal); the title list and the generic-role drop list; the 30 a day cap; retention: a found person's name and address stay until their candidate or the contact or Firm admitted from it is deleted (no scheduled sweep; an expired person is merely inadmissible).

## Consequences and limits

Volume depends on Apollo coverage of small property managers, which is unknown; many companies will yield nobody. Name accuracy rests on the provider: `Hi {first_name}` greets whoever the provider says holds the title today. Provider data can be stale (the person left); replies and bounces are the signal. The preview shows provenance; the contact row is the only place the provider's answer is kept, and no raw body is stored. Rotating the key is a secret-value change with a worker restart.
