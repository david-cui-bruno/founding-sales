## Supported patterns, limited to `pavankumarny`

1. **Hook then reversal or meaning.** A setback becomes launch momentum (P1); an emotional negative becomes a customer-win pitch (P5); a numerical publishing target becomes a personal lesson (P2). The technique is observable; its effect on reach is unknown.
2. **Short-line, spoken rhythm.** Most paragraphs carry one sentence or fragment. First-person founder narration, repeated sentence openings and emphatic future statements recur across P1–P6. Specific personal circumstances precede broad motivational takeaways in P2/P6.
3. **Founder journey is the content engine.** Hackathons, team, accelerator milestones, moving city and persistence recur (P1/P3/P4/P6). Product positioning is compressed into a sentence rather than exhaustively explained. This sample is not evidence of a technical tutorial strategy.
4. **Personal artifacts and association, not demonstrated product mechanics.** Captions identify a letter, team/encounter photos and working scenes. Named teammates/institutions supply context (P1/P3/P6). None of this sample establishes that product demos or charts are the dominant media format; P5's result anecdote is not an independently verified case study.
5. **Mixed distribution asks.** Numbered series labels connect posts over time (P1/P2/P5); named people and organizations are linked (P1/P3/P6); direct trial/demo, DM, tagging and comment-for-access asks vary by post. P5 adds hashtags. We observed these public tactics, not a scheduler, a posting-time strategy, cross-platform syndication, a conversion funnel's performance, or a reply automation system.

No engagement metrics are used to rank posts or infer causation. No claim is made that formatting, tags or daily posting caused business outcomes.
