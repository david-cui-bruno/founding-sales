# Research #619: 20 vs. 30 minutes for an initial founder-led call

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-13. **Context:** #619 supports #609 and is mapped under #606. **Scope:** initial PM intro/discovery, not enterprise demo or deep qualification. Public primary sources only.

## Evidence

| Primary source | Explicit duration and context | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Cindy Alvarez, “A different talk, each time” (May 15, 2011): https://www.cindyalvarez.com/a-different-talk-each-time/ | For customer-development interviews, a five-question script plus improvised follow-ups can provoke **20 minutes of conversation**. | Direct support for a 20-minute discovery conversation; practical prescription, not outcome comparison. |
| Cindy Alvarez, “Ask Cindy: How can we make sure we aren't just coming away from our customer conversations with a list of features?” (September 7, 2021): https://www.cindyalvarez.com/ask-cindy-how-can-we-make-sure-we-arent-just-coming-away-from-our-customer-conversations-with-a-list-of-features/ | Example framing gives **20 minutes to problem discussion**, then reserves time for status updates and other topics. | Twenty minutes is a segment, not total call length; no 20-vs-30 comparison. |
| Pete Kazanjy, *Founding Sales*, Chapter 5: https://www.foundingsales.com/5-outreach | In outbound contact, seeks **10–20 minutes** to understand pains and discuss a potential solution. For a spontaneous “hot transfer,” it describes a **30-minute demo-lite** with relevant discovery. | Context-dependent sales appointment-setting scripts, not one uniform prescription for an initial PM discovery call or evidence 30 is better. | Pete Kazanjy, *Founding Sales*, Chapter 6: https://www.foundingsales.com/6-inbound-marketing | Separates lightweight inbound lead qualification from subsequent demo/discovery; no default call duration found in the reviewed section. | Supports distinguishing qualification from demo, not a duration choice. |
| Steve Blank, customer-discovery toolkit: https://steveblank.com/tools-and-blogs-for-entrepreneurs/ | First-party material addresses interview conduct, flow, problem understanding, and avoiding “Death by Demo”; reviewed page gives no 20- or 30-minute recommendation. | Helps keep discovery distinct from demo; no duration evidence. |

**Comparison result:** No genuine head-to-head evidence comparing outcomes of otherwise equivalent initial calls booked for 20 versus 30 minutes was found in this bounded review. These are practical formats/scripts, not experiments; they do not establish conversion, learning, or a booking optimum. Kazanjy's stated time-blocking buffer is separate from call duration and does not justify changing the currently proposed **10-minute** buffer.

## Limited conclusion and proposed format

Retain **20 minutes** for the initial PM intro/discovery call as a reasonable, preference-aligned choice, not a definitive research winner. Alvarez directly supports this format; Kazanjy's sources show durations vary with the job (brief outbound invitation, scheduled follow-up/demo, or hot-transfer demo-lite), rather than proving a single universally best length. Neither establishes that 30 minutes improves an initial PM discovery conversation.

Suggested agenda: **0–2** introductions, purpose, permission; **2–4** PM context and question to learn; **4–14** recent workflow, concrete example, pain/current workaround; **14–18** impact, what has been tried, useful follow-up; **18–20** recap, correction, mutually agreed next step. Keep it discovery-led; do not force in a demo.

If more time seems useful, ask whether both parties wish to continue, check David's authorized calendar and confirm the prospect has time, and extend only by agreement. Otherwise schedule a follow-up. Keep the proposed 10-minute buffer separate from call time; it still needs David's confirmation.

**Boundary:** Research recommendation only. No final human approval, calendar booking/change, or outreach authorization is inferred. No 20-vs-30 causal or conversion claim is made.
