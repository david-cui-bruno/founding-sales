# Attorney Consult Agenda — Callie Lead Sourcing System (1 hour)

Scope: outbound B2B sales system targeting Rhode Island rental property owners/landlords, sourced from public records (RI Rental Registry, municipal tax rolls), enriched via skip-trace vendors, contacted by founder-dialed cold calls and email. No automated dialing, no prerecorded voice, no data resale.

Time budget: ~10 min per question, 10 min buffer for follow-ups.

## Q1. RI DTPPA scope — are landlords "consumers"? (~10 min)

The RI Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act (R.I. Gen. Laws § 6-48.1, effective Jan 1, 2026) protects "customers" acting in an individual or household context. We process data about self-managing landlords, many of whom are individuals or sole proprietors, strictly for B2B outreach about property-management software.

- Are individuals acting in a commercial capacity (owning/leasing rental property) outside the Act's protected scope, even when the registry lists a home address?
- We are likely below the applicability thresholds (35,000 RI customers, or 10,000 plus >20% revenue from data sale). What minimum compliance posture do you recommend below thresholds, given RI's reputation for strict enforcement and no cure period?
- Does anything in our sourcing (public government records) qualify for the publicly-available-information carve-out?

## Q2. Skip-trace vendor tier — proper use (~10 min)

We plan to use Tracerfy and/or REISkip on their non-FCRA "marketing" tier to find phone numbers and emails for landlords identified in public records, for B2B cold outreach only.

- Confirm this is a proper use of the non-FCRA tier (no eligibility decisions: no credit, employment, tenancy, or insurance determinations).
- What representations in vendor terms should we affirmatively avoid, and what should our internal use policy say so we never drift into FCRA-regulated territory?
- Any issue with combining skip-trace output with registry data and retaining it for 12 months?

## Q3. Call recording — RI, MA, CT (~10 min)

Founder wants to record his own sales calls for coaching and note-taking.

- Confirm RI is one-party consent (R.I. Gen. Laws § 11-35-21) so recording our own calls to RI numbers is fine.
- MA is all-party consent under its wiretap statute (M.G.L. c. 272 § 99). When calling into MA numbers, is a recorded pre-call disclosure ("this call may be recorded") plus continued participation sufficient consent, or must we get express assent? Practical script language?
- CT: Conn. Gen. Stat. § 52-570d requires all-party consent (or beep/notice) for recording telephone calls, with civil liability; the criminal eavesdropping statute is one-party. What posture covers CT safely?
- Given mixed-state calling, is the simplest policy "announce recording on every call" and does that fully resolve the issue?

## Q4. TCPA and telemarketing registration — RI/MA/CT (~10 min)

Founder will manually dial cold calls, including to cell phones. No ATDS, no prerecorded or artificial voice, no texts initially.

- Confirm manual dialing to cell phones without prior consent is permissible under the TCPA (no ATDS/prerecorded-voice trigger), subject to DNC rules.
- Do B2B-only calls to sole-proprietor landlords count as "calls to consumers" for federal Telemarketing Sales Rule / DNC purposes? (FTC treats sole proprietors' numbers as registrable.)
- State registration: RI's Telephone Sales Solicitation Act (R.I. Gen. Laws § 5-61-3) requires telemarketers to register with the RI AG ($100/yr, 10 days before doing business, and § 5-61-3.1 mentions a bond). Does a single founder dialing his own B2B calls fall under this, or does an exemption apply? Same question for MA's telephone solicitor registration (M.G.L. c. 159C; 201 CMR 12.00, OCABR annual registration) and CT's telemarketing chapter (Conn. Gen. Stat. ch. 743m, §§ 42-284 et seq.).
- If registration is required anywhere, can you handle the filings, and what do they cost all-in?

## Q5. CTDPA "sale" trigger and our data practices (~10 min)

Connecticut's CTDPA applies at lower thresholds when a controller derives revenue from the "sale" of personal data, and "sale" is defined broadly (exchange for monetary or other valuable consideration).

- We never sell or share data for consideration: public-record sourcing, internal use only, 12-month retention if uncontacted, permanent suppression list for opt-outs. Confirm nothing in this flow (including skip-trace vendor relationships) could be construed as a "sale."
- Below CTDPA thresholds (35,000 CT consumers as of the 2026 amendments [VERIFY: current CT threshold figure after 2025 amendments effective July 1, 2026]), what obligations if any still apply?
- Does the same analysis hold for the RI DTPPA's data-sale disclosure provisions, which reportedly apply without a minimum threshold to anyone who sells data?
