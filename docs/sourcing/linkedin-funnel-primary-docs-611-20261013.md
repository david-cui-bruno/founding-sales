# LinkedIn funnel research: primary documentation (decision #611)

**Accessed 2026-10-13. Scope:** current public LinkedIn help/legal docs and Botdog's own public help/docs only. This is documentation research, not an account/provider test. David's stated written permission FROM LinkedIn covering the full browser workflow is accepted as the premise, not independently verified. It is not merely permission from an account owner, and no scan is required. Concrete business policy and observed implementation/readiness remain separate. No LinkedIn or Botdog account was accessed.

## Five load-bearing findings

1. **Default restrictions and separately written platform permission are distinct.** LinkedIn's User Agreement, effective November 3, 2025, introduces section8 with an explicit exception for activities separately permitted by LinkedIn in writing. Section8.2 otherwise restricts unauthorized automation for contacts/messages/posts. Its general help page describes default restrictions and possible account consequences. The coordinator rechecked the section8 exception on2026-10-13. This project proceeds on David's statement that LinkedIn supplied the broad written exception, without demanding a scan or claiming to have verified its contents. Default-policy documentation is not a new paperwork blocker; implementation, business rules and observed account restrictions remain separate. [LinkedIn User Agreement, §8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement) (effective 2025-11-03); [Prohibited software and extensions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1341387/prohibition-of-scraping-software?intendedLocale=en&lang=en-us).

2. **There is no authoritative public numeric connection quota or safety threshold in the reviewed LinkedIn invitation guidance.** LinkedIn says limits vary/are enforced, and restrictions can follow many invites in a short time, ignored/pending/spam-reported invites, or suspected automation. LinkedIn advises inviting people known and trusted; it says outstanding-invitation restrictions may mean waiting up to a month and a withdrawn invite cannot be re-sent to that same person for up to three weeks. Do not encode vendor quotas as LinkedIn guarantees. [Types of restrictions for sending invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/types-of-restrictions-for-sending-invitations) (last updated ~6 months before access); [ways to connect](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a541669/inviting-or-connecting-with-people-on-linkedin?lang=en) (last updated ~5 months before access).

3. **Invitation, message, and post limits must not be collapsed into one daily-action budget.** LinkedIn says invitation limits/restrictions are separate for standard and personalized invitations; messages depend on connection status and recipient settings; it publishes no universal safe daily DM count in the reviewed help pages. It warns that high-volume messages, comments, posts, or other content in a short period can lead to reduced visibility or account restrictions, without giving a safe threshold. Page scheduling is a first-party feature for eligible Page admins, one hour to three months ahead; member posts can also be scheduled, but LinkedIn may limit posting frequency. [Invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/types-of-restrictions-for-sending-invitations); [messaging FAQ](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a550206/linkedin-messaging-faq?intendedLocale=en&lang=en-us); [high volume of content shared](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339697/high-volume-of-messages-sent?lang=en); [scheduled Page posts](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548192/scheduled-posts-for-linkedin-pages?lang=en); [post and share updates](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a527226?hcppcid=search).

4. **Botdog's limit figures are vendor estimates/inconsistent claims, not authoritative LinkedIn limits.** Its help page recommends 5–10 daily invites for free accounts and 25 for Premium, based on claimed weekly 50/150 figures. Another Botdog help article says typically 50 free / 150–200 Premium; Botdog FAQ says 100–200 weekly. Its own 2025 blog says Premium does not raise the limit and gives different counts (free up to 100; Premium 150–200), while other vendor material makes still other claims. These discrepancies, plus LinkedIn's non-public, account-dependent restrictions, mean none is suitable as a guaranteed threshold or “ban-proof” operating rate. Botdog claims that delays, work-hours distribution, or cloud operation help avoid detection are vendor claims, not LinkedIn authorization or proof of safe operation. [Botdog daily limits](https://help.botdog.co/en/articles/11964644-setting-your-daily-linkedin-action-limits); [Botdog troubleshooting](https://kb.botdog.co/en/article/why-are-my-invitations-or-messages-not-sending-1l23fag/); [Botdog FAQ](https://www.botdog.co/faq); [Botdog premium-limit blog](https://www.botdog.co/blog-posts/linkedin-premium-connection-request-limit); [Botdog safety article](https://help.botdog.co/en/articles/11964701-linkedin-account-safety-automating-with-confidence-using-botdog).

5. **Replies and opt-outs require a hard stop/human policy, not just sequence logic.** Botdog documents a default messaging condition that pauses a sequence after an unexpected message, including a prospect's reply, but says this behavior can be changed; this is not proof of reliable real-time detection or opt-out classification. Botdog also describes reply webhooks, but that is a notification/integration claim, not verified delivery or stop latency. LinkedIn prohibits spam, irrelevant/unwanted or gratuitously repetitive messages and says recipients can report messages; platform warnings/restrictions require stopping rather than retrying or working around them. **Recommendation (inference):** suppress further outbound actions immediately on any reply, opt-out, complaint, warning/challenge, or ambiguous execution result; route substantive replies to David; do not auto-send a reply or replay a request when outcome is uncertain. [Botdog sequence stop behavior](https://kb.botdog.co/en/article/when-botdog-sequences-stop-and-when-they-dont-1h3ry2x/); [Botdog reply webhook guide](https://kb.botdog.co/en/article/connect-botdog-to-slack-k7i9qj/); [Professional Community Policies](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/professional-community-policies); [message reporting](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551431/report-message-content?lang=en); [account restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1340522).

## Scope notes and recommendations (inferences)

- LinkedIn invitation guidance says excessive pending/ignored invitations can contribute to restrictions; it does not disclose a generally safe pending-count ceiling. Botdog's advertised “under 500” / 500–700 figures are its own heuristics, while its auto-withdraw page promotes 30 days and another article says a withdrawn request can be resent after 30 days. LinkedIn's help instead says up to three weeks after withdrawal, and separately suggests waiting up to a month when restricted for too many outstanding invitations. These are different situations; prefer LinkedIn's restriction-specific guidance and do not automatically withdraw/re-invite as a quota workaround. [LinkedIn invitation restrictions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551012/types-of-restrictions-for-sending-invitations); [Botdog auto-withdraw](https://www.botdog.co/features/auto-withdraw); [Botdog invitation-limit article](https://kb.botdog.co/en/article/youve-reached-the-weekly-linkedin-invitation-limit-what-it-means-and-what-to-do-1l7vmz2/).
- LinkedIn's scheduling documentation describes a native capability, not third-party authorization by itself. The broad stated written exception is the project's permission premise. David chose once-approved standing business policies, not human review of every post or recipient. Inference: bind every action to that versioned policy, current account/content and action-specific limits; use existing native/browser seams where they satisfy the chosen contract. Native scheduling, connections and DMs still have separate observed-result and uncertainty checks.
- The user's permission premise is accepted as instructed. It is not independently verified, and no scan is requested. It does not establish which audience, post/message content, cadence, budget, or booking-only logistics are approved.

## Remaining unknowns and proof limits

- LinkedIn does not publish a stable universal connection-request or DM safe-rate in the reviewed material; it gives no dependable numeric point at which automation becomes safe. Never promise ban-proof safety.
- Public docs do not establish Botdog's actual account-specific behavior, precise hours/time zone, opt-out recognition, warning/challenge detection, action reconciliation, cancellation race guarantees, or uncertain-send recovery. Documentation claims are not controlled tests/provider acceptance.
- This track does not assess current source implementation, installed build, account configuration, owner consent record, credential/access boundaries, or production readiness. No product or operational approval follows from this memo.
