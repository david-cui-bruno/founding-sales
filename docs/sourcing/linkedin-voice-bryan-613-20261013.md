## Observed patterns (small-sample hypotheses, not a style law)

- **Hooks:** assertive contrast (“product-first” vs. “research-first”; users vs. research), or a high-stakes personal decision followed by a launch. The endorsement instead starts with its verdict.
- **Rhythm and breaks:** short declarative clauses and fragments create pace; longer paragraphs carry explanation. Line breaks isolate pivots and punch lines. Rendered text sometimes collapses breaks, so the displayed breaks are evidence only where visible.
- **Story structure:** decision/change → specific stakes → problem → what was built → evidence/context → invitation. Opinion posts tend toward claim → examples → operating principle → question or conversation CTA.
- **Specificity:** concrete people, product, quantities, time spans, tools, and user jobs make claims legible. Examples in posts are Bryan's claims, not independently verified outcomes here.
- **Promotion and CTA:** explicitly promotional, but often preceded by a point of view or explanation. CTAs seek relevant conversations or product interest; the short endorsement is intentionally just endorsement. No inference is made from reactions/comments to conversion or business results.

## Transfer to David's original posts

Borrow the **craft**, not Bryan's sentences, anecdotes, claims, or results: open with one honest observation or a real maintenance/building change; explain the problem in plain language; give one or two verifiable specifics; use breaks to make the reasoning easy to scan; end with a useful question or low-pressure invitation only when genuine. Rotate building updates, maintenance, news, and technology topics. David approved **two text-only posts weekly**. Do not invent personal experience, positions, results, or promises, and do not imply an outcome unsupported by David's own facts.
