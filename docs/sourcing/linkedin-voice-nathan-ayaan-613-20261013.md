## Voice and craft patterns observed

- **Hooks:** Leung tends toward a real work problem or specific update; Gazali more often uses direct address, self-deprecation, or a strong thesis. Both make the subject legible immediately.
- **Rhythm and format:** Leung’s examples are concise, organized around concrete facts, a few alternatives or bullets, and a link/next step. Gazali uses longer, denser first-person arcs, rhetorical turns, lists, and explicit platform/audience framing. These are tendencies in this sample, not universal rules.
- **Technical specificity:** Leung names implementation choices and their rationale, including a caveat that his route is not the only solution. Gazali names research fields and describes intended explanation formats, but the sampled announcement itself does not substantiate technical claims with demonstrations.
- **Humor and story:** Leung’s conference-giveaway details add gentle levity; the architecture post stays sober. Gazali’s “problem” hook and “losing aura” aside deliberately play with online self-presentation. His origin-story details belong to him and are not templates for David to imitate.
- **CTAs:** Observed forms include visit/meet at an event, read linked architecture/notes, comment a keyword, or follow elsewhere. CTA presence is a writing choice, not evidence that engagement caused leads, sales, or conversion.

## Safe transfer to David’s original posts

For David’s approved two text-only posts weekly, use the mechanics, not their stories or claims: open with one true, bounded update, maintenance lesson, relevant news item, or technical question; give a short sequence of concrete context and reasoning; close with a modest next step or genuine question only when useful. Keep technical claims within what David can substantiate; distinguish observation from opinion, include uncertainty/tradeoffs where relevant, and use a light human detail only if it is David’s own. Do not borrow anecdotes, phrases, milestones, metrics, results, or unheld opinions from these authors. Do not imply customer outcomes, make private customer stories public, or promise product capabilities or delivery. No engagement-to-conversion inference.
