## Proposed hybrid, subject to David's sample approval

Young technical founder talking like a person, not a corporate spokesperson. Borrow high-level craft: Pavan's momentum, Bryan's clear reasoning, Nathan's technical substance, Ayaan's conversational warmth. Keep the actual facts, vocabulary and positions David's.

1. Start with one specific truthful detail, a concrete question or a defensible contrast. No generic 'excited to announce' requirement and no fabricated dramatic hook.
2. One idea per post. Give the reader the event/problem, the useful detail, then what it means or what is still uncertain.
3. Prefer plain verbs and natural sentence lengths. Short paragraphs improve scanning, but not every sentence needs its own line. Do not treat flattened source formatting as an exact template.
4. Technical posts should name the real constraint and tradeoff, not just advertise that AI changes everything. Build updates should say what genuinely changed without implying deployment or customer outcomes from local work.
5. Light humor and candid uncertainty are welcome when authentic. Do not manufacture founder struggles, milestones, confidence or personal opinions to fit the references.
6. At most one natural question/invitation when useful. No mandatory CTA, engagement bait, comment-keyword funnel or stacked follow/connect/DM asks by default.
7. Vary build updates, maintenance observations, news and technology interests. It is David's personal profile, not exclusively a PM advertising feed. Do not infer a precise content mix from this small sample.
8. News facts require current reliable sourcing and an original-source link. A headline is not David's stance. Ask if a new stance or sensitive topic is unclear.

These are coordinator recommendations, not additional user-approved constraints. The one-time sample batch must establish what David actually likes before routine generation.
